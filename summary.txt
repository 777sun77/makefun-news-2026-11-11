Wednesday, Nov. 11: war, disease and climate strain global agenda

Russia and Ukraine resumed U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi for a second day. Kyiv raised prisoner and civilian returns and security guarantees, but neither side signaled a compromise. Overnight Russian strikes wounded seven people, while drones set fire to the Ust-Luga oil port.

At COP31 in Antalya, leaders began two days of speeches as nearly 200 countries faced pressure to turn climate pledges into action. Turkey wants electricity to account for 35% of global energy use by 2035, but its plan does not require clean power. The U.S. again stayed away.

The Iran war added to regional tensions: Israeli strikes and gunfire killed five people in Gaza on Tuesday despite the ceasefire. Iran said three ships hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz; a tanker was struck off Oman, and Abu Dhabi’s oil company reported an attack on a vessel in Gulf waters.

Congo’s Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak topped 11,000 infections and 5,387 deaths as fighting and attacks on health workers hindered containment. Kenya discharged its last patient, starting a 42-day countdown to Ebola-free status; Uganda and Nigeria also reported imported cases.

South Sudan’s planned Dec. 22 election will test a fragile peace. AU envoy Jakaya Kikwete urged the U.N. Security Council to help prevent violence, warning that a flawed vote could trigger a wider crisis after years of conflict.

House Republicans blocked Rep. Thomas Massie’s bid to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over his leadership of the Iran war. The vote exposed Republican strains after midterm losses partly blamed on the war and fuel prices; Democrats take control in January.

Markets diverged as AI optimism lifted chip shares: Taiwan’s Taiex rose 3.6% and South Korea’s Kospi 2.4% on chip demand. Hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation fueled bets the Fed may raise rates in December, while Brent crude hovered near $92 a barrel.
