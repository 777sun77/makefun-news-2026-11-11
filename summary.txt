Wednesday, Nov. 11: talks resume amid war, disease and climate pressure

Russia and Ukraine opened a second day of U.S.-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi. Kyiv raised prisoner and civilian returns and security guarantees, but neither side signaled compromise. Overnight Russian strikes wounded seven, and drones set fire to the Ust-Luga oil port.

At COP31 in Antalya, leaders began two days of speeches as nearly 200 countries face pressure to turn climate pledges into action. Turkey wants electricity to reach 35% of global energy use by 2035, but its plan does not require clean power. The U.S. again stayed away.

The Iran war raised pressure on regional shipping: Israeli strikes and gunfire killed five in Gaza Tuesday despite the ceasefire. Iran said three ships hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz; a tanker was struck off Oman, and Abu Dhabi’s oil company reported an attack on a vessel in Gulf waters.

Congo’s Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak topped 11,000 infections and 5,387 deaths, while fighting and attacks on health workers hindered containment. Kenya discharged its last patient and began a 42-day countdown to Ebola-free status; Uganda and Nigeria also reported imported cases.

South Sudan’s planned Dec. 22 election tests a fragile peace. AU envoy Jakaya Kikwete urged the U.N. Security Council to help prevent violence, warning a flawed vote could trigger a larger crisis after years of conflict.

House Republicans blocked Rep. Thomas Massie’s bid to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over his Iran-war leadership. The vote exposed Republican strains after midterm losses partly blamed on the war and fuel prices; Democrats take control in January.

Markets split over AI optimism and inflation: Taiwan’s Taiex rose 3.6% and South Korea’s Kospi 2.4% on chip demand, while hotter-than-expected U.S. inflation fueled bets the Fed may raise rates in December. Brent crude hovered near $92 a barrel.
